In short: everyone gets the same secret word on their own phone, except the Mole, whose phone shows a card that says “YOU’RE THE MOLE!” and no word. Each player says one clue out loud, then everyone votes on their phone. The Mole has to blend in with vague clues; if voted out, the Mole gets one last guess at the word to steal the win. For a twist, add an optional Undercover: a player who gets a different word and doesn’t know it.

Mish Ana! (Lebanese for “Not me!”) is played with one TV and one phone per player, for 3 to 12 players. A round takes a few minutes and the first one teaches the rules. This page covers everything: setup, roles, a full round, ties, how the game ends, points and tips for each role.

What you need

  • A TV screen: the Mish Ana! app on an Android TV or Google TV, or the browser versionthe browser version ({{BROWSER_SHORT}}) on a laptop connected to any TV. The Android TV / Google TV app is coming soon to Google Play.
  • One phone per player, iPhone or Android, with a camera and internet. Nothing to install: the game opens in the phone’s browser.
  • 3 to 12 players in the same room. The game is spoken: clues are said out loud.

Setup in 30 seconds

  1. Open Mish Ana! on the TV. It shows a QR code and a four-letter room code.
  2. Everyone scans the QR code with their phone camera, types a nickname and picks a colour.
  3. The host (the TV, or the first player) checks the settings and presses Start.

The roles

Each phone shows either the secret word or the Mole card. The words are easy, everyday ones: moon, escalator, Paris, forest, pizza, coffee.

Civilian

Has the secret word, for example PIZZA. Goal: find and vote out the Mole without giving the word away.

The Mole

Gets no word. Their phone shows “YOU’RE THE MOLE!”, so the Mole knows. Goal: listen, give vague clues that sound right and survive. If voted out, the Mole gets one last guess at the word: a correct guess steals the win.

The Undercover (optional)

Gets a different word, for example PASTA while everyone else has PIZZA, and isn’t told it is different. Goal: work out from the clues that their word is the odd one, then blend in and survive. Off by default; the host can add Undercovers, or mix them with the Mole.

How many Moles and Undercovers?

The default game has one Mole and no Undercover. The host can change the mix in the settings: add Undercovers, play with Undercovers only, or mix both roles, always with fewer infiltrators than Civilians.

A round, step by step

  1. Secret words. Each phone shows a face-down card. Press and hold to see your word (or the Mole card), let go to hide it. Your card never appears on the TV while you play.
  2. Clues. The TV picks who starts and shows whose turn it is. Each player says one word or a short phrase out loud that relates to the word, without saying the word. The Mole has to bluff one too.
  3. Vote. Everyone votes on their phone for the player who sounds off. You can’t vote for yourself.
  4. Reveal. The player with the most votes is out. The TV stamps them OUT and reveals their role: Civilian, Mole or Undercover.
  5. Next round. If nobody has won yet, the remaining players give new clues and vote again.

Ties

If two or more players tie for the most votes, only they give one more clue each, then everyone votes again between them. If the re-vote ties again, the game picks one of them at random (the host can change this to “nobody is out”).

The Mole’s last guess

When the Mole is voted out, they type one guess at the word on their phone. Spelling doesn’t need to be perfect, and the host can accept or reject a close answer. Right: the Mole steals the win. Wrong: the Civilians are one step closer.

How the game ends

  • Civilians win when every Mole and Undercover has been voted out and no last guess was right.
  • The Mole steals the win by guessing the word after being voted out.
  • The infiltrators win (Moles and Undercovers together) when too few Civilians are left to outvote them; the host picks the exact win rule.

Scoring

Every player on the winning side scores, even if they were voted out earlier. Infiltrators score more than Civilians, because they win less often.

Scores carry over when you press Play again, so a party night becomes a league. The host can set each value.

Tips for each role

As a Civilian

  • Give clues that prove you know the word without making it easy to guess. For PIZZA, “slice” is fine; “Italian round thing with cheese” hands the word to the Mole.
  • Listen for clues that could fit almost anything. “Fork” or “tasty” says nothing: that’s how the Mole sounds.
  • Don’t rush the first vote: a vague clue can come from a careful Civilian.

As the Mole

  • Try not to speak early. Build your clue from the ones you’ve heard.
  • Stay general (“I’d have it on a Friday night”) and keep a guess ready in case you’re caught.
  • Vote with the group when it’s safe: a lone vote draws attention.

As the Undercover

  • You don’t know you’re the Undercover. If the first clues feel slightly off for your word, you probably are.
  • Pick clues that fit both your word and the one you suspect the others have.

Settings worth knowing

  • Roles: the classic Mole game, Undercovers only, or both mixed.
  • Beginner mode shows everyone their role, which is great for a first game with kids or newcomers.
  • Family-friendly filter keeps only word packs rated for everyone.
  • Timers for clues, votes and the Mole’s last guess, or none: the host moves things on.
  • Word packs in English, French and Arabic, including Lebanese packs.
