If you like Spyfall or Undercover, you like hidden-role word games: one or more players don’t share the group’s information and must bluff. Spyfall, Undercover and The Chameleon are played around a table or on phones. To play this kind of game on a TV, with each player’s secret on their own phone, the options are Mish Ana! (secret-word game for Android TV, Google TV and browsers, 3–12 players, free to play) and the social-deduction games in some Jackbox party packs (paid).

What these games have in common

Everyone gets a secret, except one or a few players who get a different secret or none. The group talks, asks or gives clues, then votes. The outsider wins by blending in or by working out the secret. The games differ in what is secret (a location, a word, a topic) and how you talk (questions, one-word clues, free discussion).

Mish Ana! (the TV option for secret-word games)

Closest to Undercover-style play, built for a shared screen. The TV shows a QR code; each player joins in their phone’s browser (nothing to install) and gets their word privately. Everyone shares a word except the Mole, whose phone says “YOU’RE THE MOLE!” and who gets one last guess at the word if caught; an optional Undercover role adds a close word the player doesn’t know is different. One spoken clue each, a vote on the phones, a reveal on the TV, and scores across games.

  • Best for: groups of 5 to 12 around one TV, families, mixed-language groups (English, French, Arabic with Lebanese packs).
  • Difference from the phone apps: no passing one phone around, and nobody has to be the game master.
  • Needs: an Android TV or Google TV, or a laptop on any TV; internet.a laptop on any TV (browser version) and internet; the Android TV / Google TV app is coming soon to Google Play.

Rules: how to play Mish Ana!

Spyfall

A card game designed by Alexandr Ushan and published by Hobby World in 2014. Everyone gets the same location card (a beach, a submarine…) except the spy. Players take turns asking each other questions; the spy tries to guess the location without being caught. Spyfall 2 (2017) raises the count to 12 players and can have two spies.

  • Best for: groups who enjoy asking clever questions; no screens.
  • Difference: questions and a location instead of one-word clues and a word.

Undercover

A phone party game by Yanstar Studio, released in 2015, with the roles Civilians, Undercover and Mr. White. It supports 3 to 20 players: pass one phone around the table, or play on your own devices in private or public online rooms. It popularised the “Mr. White” name for the no-word role. Official rules.

  • Best for: playing anywhere with just a phone.
  • Difference: phone-first; we couldn’t find a TV version as of October 2026.

The Chameleon

A card game by Rikki Tahta, published by Big Potato in 2017, for 3 to 8 players. A topic card shows 16 words; dice pick the secret word for everyone except the Chameleon. Each player says one word, then the group votes. A caught Chameleon can still win by guessing the secret word. More on Wikipedia.

  • Best for: quick rounds; very close to Mish Ana!’s Mole, who also gets a last guess.

Codenames

Not a hidden-role game, but a favourite with the same crowd. Two teams; a spymaster gives one-word clues so their team finds their agents on a grid of words. Designed by Vlaada Chvátil (Czech Games Edition), with a free official browser version you can put on a TV through a laptop.

Jackbox: Fakin’ It and Push The Button

Two hidden-role games inside paid Jackbox party packs, which run on Android TV with phones as controllers: Fakin’ It (Party Pack 3), where one player doesn’t know the task and must fake it, and Push The Button (Party Pack 6), where players hunt for aliens among the crew. Each pack also has four other games. Party Pack 3 costs $24.99 and Party Pack 6 $29.99 on Google Play in the US (Pack 3, Pack 6).

  • Best for: groups who already like Jackbox and want a bluffing round.
  • Difference: one game among five in a paid pack; English-only in those packs.

Werewolf / Mafia

The folk classic: a narrator secretly assigns werewolves and villagers; at night the werewolves pick a victim, by day the village votes. Needs 7+ players and a narrator who doesn’t play. Free, no equipment.

Which one should you pick?

  • You have a TV and 5–12 people: Mish Ana! (free to play) or a Jackbox pack.
  • No screens, 3–8 people: Spyfall or The Chameleon.
  • Only one phone: a pass-the-phone app like Undercover.
  • Teams rather than bluffing: Codenames.
  • A big group, no equipment: Werewolf.
Is there a Spyfall game for the TV? Spyfall itself is a card game. For a similar hidden-role game on a TV, Mish Ana! runs on Android TV, Google TV and in any browserin any browser on the TV screen (Google TV app coming soon), with players using their own phones; the difference is that the secret is a word and players give one clue each instead of asking questions. What’s the difference between Spyfall and Undercover? In Spyfall, everyone but the spy knows a location and players ask each other questions. In Undercover-style games, players get words, one has a close word and one may have none, and everyone gives a one-word clue before voting. Is Mish Ana! the same as Undercover? No. It’s an independent game in the same genre. Its roles are the Civilians, the Mole (no word, one last guess if caught) and an optional Undercover (close word, not told), and it is built for a TV with phones as controllers. What’s a free game like Spyfall for a big group? Werewolf needs no equipment. Mish Ana! is free to play for 3 to 12 players with one TV or laptop and a phone each. Codenames has a free official browser version for team play.
