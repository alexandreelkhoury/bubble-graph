(Lebanese for “Not me!”) does that on the TV: 3 to 12 players, everyone on their own phone, in Arabic, English or French, with Lebanese word packs like falafel or shawarma.

Why a TV game works for a sahra

A Lebanese sahra is rarely four people. It’s cousins, neighbours, Teta on the sofa and someone who just dropped by. Card games and tawleh are great, but they seat two to four players while everyone else watches. A party game on the TV lets the whole living room play together, and every phone in the room is already a controller.

Game night plan (about 2 hours)

  1. Arrival and mezze: tawleh in the corner for whoever wants it.
  2. Warm-up round on the TV: one game of Mish Ana! in Beginner mode so everyone sees their role and learns the rules.
  3. The real games: switch Beginner mode off. Scores carry over between games, so keep a running league for the night.
  4. Cards for the die-hards: tarneeb or trix at the table while the others keep playing on the TV.
  5. Final: one last game, highest score picks the dessert.

The secret-word game, Lebanese style

Everyone gets a secret word on their phone. Most players get the same word, the Mole gets a close one without knowing it, and in bigger groups the Blank gets nothing and bluffs. One clue each, a vote on the phones, and the TV reveals who was who. The full rules are on the how to play page.

Words that start arguments

Two Lebanese packs (Lebanese food and Lebanese life, written in Arabic script) use the words people actually say. Pairs like falafel / shawarma, tabbouleh / fattoush, servees / taxi or kahraba / motor are close enough that the Mole won’t notice, and every clue becomes a debate. There’s even trix / likha for the card players.

Mixed-language groups

  • The host picks the word language for the room: Arabic, English or French.
  • The game interface is available in all three, and the Arabic version runs fully right to left, on the TV and on phones.
  • A family where the kids prefer English and the grown-ups Arabic can still play together: choose the word language the whole group knows best.

Tips for family and kids

  • Turn on the family-friendly filter so only packs rated for everyone are used.
  • Use Beginner mode for the first game: everyone sees their role.
  • Pair a younger child with a grown-up on one phone if they don’t read yet.
  • No accounts, no ads: players only type a nickname, and rooms are deleted automatically.
