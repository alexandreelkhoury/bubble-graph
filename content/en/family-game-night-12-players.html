The best games for a big family night let everyone play at once, need almost nothing, and teach themselves in one round. For up to 12 players, good picks are charades and fishbowl (teams, no equipment), werewolf / mafia (7+ players with a narrator), and a TV party game like Mish Ana!, a secret-word game where all 12 players use their own phone and the TV runs the game.

1. A secret-word game on the TV: Mish Ana!

Everyone gets a secret word on their own phone. Most players share the same word; the Mole has a slightly different one and doesn’t know it, and from 5 players a Blank has no word and bluffs. One clue each, a vote on the phones, and the TV reveals who was who. With 12 players there are 7 Civilians, 3 Moles and 2 Blanks, which makes for loud debates.

  • Why it works for families: no one has to be the host or the referee, phones join by scanning a QR code (iPhone or Android, nothing to install), and scores carry over game after game.
  • For kids: turn on the family-friendly filter and Beginner mode (everyone sees their role). Younger kids can share a phone with a grown-up.
  • Languages: English, French and Arabic, with Lebanese word packs.
  • Needs: an Android TV or Google TV, or any TV with a laptop for the browser version, and internet.

Rules in detail: how to play Mish Ana!

2. Charades

Split into two teams. Each team writes words or film titles on slips for the other team. One player acts out a slip without speaking while their team guesses before the timer runs out (a minute works well). Kids love acting; grandparents love guessing.

3. Fishbowl (also called Celebrity)

Everyone writes three names or things on slips and drops them in a bowl. Teams play three rounds with the same slips: describe it in words, then one word only, then mime it. By round three, everyone knows the answers and the inside jokes.

4. Werewolf / Mafia

A narrator secretly hands out roles: a few werewolves (or mafia), a seer, and villagers. At “night” everyone closes their eyes and the werewolves pick a victim; by “day” the village debates and votes someone out. It shines with 8 to 12 players. The narrator doesn’t play, so rotate the role.

5. Two truths and a lie

Each player says three statements about themselves, two true and one false. The others vote on the lie. Easy with any age, and surprisingly revealing in families.

6. Wink murder

One player is secretly the murderer and “kills” others by winking at them; a detective tries to catch them. Great for young children because there is nothing to read or remember.

7. Drawing telephone

Each player writes a phrase at the top of a sheet and passes it on. The next player draws it, folds the phrase away, and passes it; the next writes what they think the drawing is, and so on. Unfold at the end.

8. Twenty questions

One player thinks of something; the others get twenty yes-or-no questions to guess it. Ideal as a warm-up while food is on the way.

Tips for a 12-player family night

  • Start with a game that teaches itself in one round, then move to longer ones.
  • Mix ages in teams rather than kids vs adults.
  • Keep rounds short (5 to 10 minutes) so people can drift in and out.
  • Keep a score for the whole night and give the winner a silly prize, like choosing dessert.
  • Have one game nobody has to referee, so the host gets to play too.
What is a good party game for 12 people? Games where everyone plays at the same time: charades or fishbowl in teams, werewolf with a narrator, or a TV party game like Mish Ana! that supports 12 players, each on their own phone, with the TV running the game. What games can kids and grandparents play together? Wink murder, charades and twenty questions need no reading or equipment.
