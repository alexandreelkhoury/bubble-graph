# Jackbox catalogue: what to build next for JAMAA (2026-11-15)

Extends [JACKBOX-STUDY.md](../JACKBOX-STUDY.md) (section 2 already lists every game and loop; section 7 lists the market gaps). This file does not repeat that catalogue. It adds popularity evidence, a fan-favourite read, and a ranked Top 5 of new JAMAA games. Existing JAMAA games: Mish Ana! (hidden word role), Bluff! (lies vs truth), Most Likely (vote on people), Quiz Night (trivia).

**Evidence limits (read first).** WebSearch worked; many sites returned 403 to fetch (Twinfinite, Digital Trends partly, Epic, Nintendo Life). Quotes marked *(snippet)* come from search-result extracts, not a full page read. Jackbox publishes no per-game play counts, and Steam rates packs, not games. I found no Reddit ranking thread (only tier-list templates), so "fan favourite" below rests on critic lists, Jackbox's own pack choices and one CEO statement. ## 2. Fan favourites, by mechanic family

Verified read: Quiplash is the clear number one; Trivia Murder Party, Fibbage and Drawful are the other pillars; the next tier is Tee K.O., Fakin' It, Survive the Internet, Patently Stupid, Champ'd Up, Joke Boat, Hypnotorious/Weapons Drawn (deduction fans) and Guesspionage. Fibbage's standing depends on the list (iconic, but some critics rate Fibbage 3/4 below Fibbage 2).

Why most of these do not fit us: adult-only games (Naughty Pack, Weapons Drawn) clash with family use; real-time reflex games need low-latency input that Wi-Fi to a Cloudflare Worker will not guarantee; heavy free typing in Arabic (Job Job, FixyText) is slow on phones.

## 3. Selection criteria

Each candidate was scored 1-3 on: **family-safe**, **laughs without skill**, **minimal typing** (Arabic keyboards are slow), **works in AR/FR/ES/EN with content we can write**, **fits phone plus D-pad TV** (the TV is passive, as in the existing games), **no overlap** with the four games, **build cost on our platform**. Constraint for all: our own names, prompts, art and voice (the study's legal section, 6.3, applies: reuse mechanics, never names or content).

Dropped: Fakin'-It/Hypnotorious/Push the Button clones (overlap with Mish Ana!), Role Models/Split the Room (overlap with Most Likely), Drawful clone (overlap with Bluff, though a drawing variant survives at #4), Dodo Re Mi/Quixort/Zeeple Dome (latency), Job Job/FixyText (Arabic typing), Naughty Pack (family).

### 4.1 #1 Zajal Battle (head-to-head wit)

*Why first.* Quiplash is the most popular Jackbox game by its CEO's own account, and it is the exact thing our Bluff-loving owner's audience will ask for next: writing, anonymity, a reveal. Short answers (3-10 words) keep Arabic typing painless, and the best answers in Arabic are often one-liners or proverb twists. Zajal is the Lebanese tradition of improvised sung duelling poetry, so the name sells the mechanic to an Arab family in two words.

*Core loop.* Each player gets two prompts; each prompt goes to exactly two players ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/jackbox-games-design-party-pack), mechanic only). The TV shows one matchup at a time, both answers anonymous; everyone else votes on their phone; the authors are revealed, points scale with vote share. Round 2 doubles; the final has every player answer one prompt and spend three votes.
*The laugh moment.* The reveal of two answers, followed by the swing of a landslide ("KO!" bonus for 100% of the vote), plus the TV reading the winning line aloud in a big Arabic or Latin display face. Two friends facing off on a prompt aimed at them is the real engine.
*Difference from our games.* Bluff has a hidden truth and players must find it; Zajal has no truth, only comparison. It reuses Bluff's anonymous-answer pipeline (moderation, script checks, reveal choreography) but not its scoring.
*Platform fit.* Reuses `matchesTruth`-style duplicate detection (identical answers score 0, as Quiplash's "Jinx"), the text widget, anonymity layer and moderation hooks from Bluff. New: pairing scheduler, vote phase, KO bonus. Odd player counts: one house answer, or a bye. 3-12 players works because the votes come from everyone else, not only the authors.
*Content.* Prompts must be written natively per language, not translated: "Something your mother says that means you are in trouble", "A bad name for a Beirut taxi company". Seed 120 per language across Family and a Spicy pack, as Most Likely already does. Reuse the `mode: family|spicy` and ageConfirmed gate.
*Risks.* Arabic wit is dialect-heavy (Levantine vs Gulf vs Egyptian), so tag prompts by dialect and let the room choose (platform content filters exist). Cheap stalling answers need a Safety answer (canned, half points, as in Quiplash). Moderation in Arabic needs a word list before streamers touch it. Shy players can freeze; keep a 60 s timer and a "pass for 0" button.

### 4.2 #2 Mamnou3! (describe it, don't say it)

*Why.* The Taboo/Alias family is already beloved in Arab living rooms, needs no typing at all, and is the most TV-and-family-friendly entry here. Jackbox's nearest ancestor, Blather 'Round, is praised as quick, easy to learn and levelling for mixed-ability groups, but is held back by 2-6 players and no strong final ([Gaming Trend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/hello-from-the-magic-box-the-jackbox-party-pack-7-review/) *(snippet)*). We fix both by using teams (so 3-12 works) and a buzz-in "guard".
*Core loop.* Two teams. A describer's phone shows the secret word and 4 forbidden words; they talk aloud (no gestures); their team shouts guesses; **a player from the other team is the guard** and their phone shows the same forbidden list with a huge BUZZ button. The describer taps "got it" or "skip" (skips cost). The TV shows only the timer, score and team colours. 60 s turns, 3 rounds; the last round is "one word only" (round-3 constraint, the genre's usual escalation).
*The laugh moment.* The guard buzzes mid-sentence, the TV does a siren sting, and the describer's face. Plus a "total fail" count at the end ("You said 'Fairuz' 3 times").
*Difference.* Mish Ana! is secret roles and one-word clues; Mamnou3! is open team shouting. It is a speaking game, so the TV and phones barely overlap with our written games.
*Platform fit.* Low server load (timers and taps). New: team assignment (the platform already has players and characters), turn rotation, the guard role. Needs the same secrecy rules as Mish Ana! (the describer's word is on one phone only).
*Content.* 250 words per language with 3-5 forbidden terms each, grouped by category (food, Lebanese places, songs, proverbs, daily life, tech). Arabic words need forbidden-word variants (stems, with and without "al"); the guard is a human, so we do not need a matcher.
*Risks.* The honour system and the guard, and in noisy rooms. Mixed-language rooms (each phone in its own UI language) are awkward when the describer's word is in another language: keep the content language per room. Translating Taboo-style lists verbatim will not work; write them natively.

### 4.3 #3 Qal el Jamaa (guess what the room said)

*Why.* Tap-only, no knowledge needed, no one is left out, and it produces conversation ("you think 70% of Lebanese answer that?"). Jackbox thought it worth three variants (Guesspionage, Poll Mine, Survey Scramble); Survey Scramble is a $9.99 standalone also sold on [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.jackboxgames.tjss&hl=en_US). Guesspionage is on [Digital Trends'](https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/best-jackbox-games) picks.
*Core loop.* A survey question ("How many people eat ka3ek on the corniche?" or "Percent who answer calls from their mother on the first ring"). A guesser slides a percentage; everyone else taps higher or lower; closest scores, correct higher/lower bets score. The final is a Poll Mine-style ranking: the room ranks five answers.
*The laugh moment.* The big reveal: the bar fills on the TV and a 3% answer that everybody swore was 60%.
*Difference.* Quiz Night has right answers with fixed facts; this one is about opinions of the crowd. Most Likely votes for people; this guesses numbers about a population.
*Platform fit.* Slider and tap widgets, TV bars. Very cheap per phase; scoring needs a simple distance function.
*Content (the real cost).* **Needs real survey data.** Plan: seed from our own in-app "ask the room" polls (a pre-game question every player answers anonymously; the game then asks "how many in this room said X?"), which needs no external data and is fun in itself. Public stat questions need citations; do not publish invented percentages as facts. 80 questions per language, native not translated.
*Risks.* Fake or stale stats embarrass us; room-based polls with 3 players are noisy (use the audience mode or require 5+ for the stat modes). Percentages are Arabic-Indic vs Western digits: platform i18n already decides this.

### 4.4 #4 Rasmet-ha (draw it, guess it)

*Why.* Drawing is the biggest laugh generator in the Jackbox catalogue (Drawful is one of the four pillars; Tee K.O. and Champ'd Up are fan favourites; [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/best-jackbox-games) lists Drawful first), it is language-neutral on the TV, and children can play. Arabic and French captions need no special support if players pick rather than type.
*Core loop.* Everyone draws a secret prompt on their phone with a twist (one stroke, no lifting, non-dominant hand, 30 s). The TV shows drawings one by one; the others **tap to pick the prompt from four options: the real one plus three taken from other players' prompts in the room** (no typing, so it does not overlap with Bluff's lie-writing). Drawer scores per correct guess; guessers score for being right. A "sketch artist" and "worst sketch" vote pays side points.
*The laugh moment.* The reveal of what each wrong guesser thought it was ("Sami was sure it was a mosque") against the real prompt.
*Difference.* Bluff is about lies you write; Rasmet-ha is about images you make and options you pick. A Drawful-style fake-caption phase is deliberately left out to avoid copying Fibbage/Drawful and overlapping with Bluff.
*Platform fit.* Biggest build: phone canvas (touch strokes, undo, clear, colour), stroke streaming or snapshot upload, TV rendering (Compose Canvas). Upload one compressed vector (path list) per drawing, not images; cap payload (HTTP body 128 KiB already exists for room creation, so check WebSocket frame limits). Our phone budget (the repo is at the 60 KB web limit) will need a lazy-loaded canvas module.
*Content.* 250 prompts per language across categories (proverbs, Lebanese places, foods, songs, TV, objects). Prompts must be drawable.
*Risks.* **Highest risk of the five**: tiny phone screens, palm and finger accuracy, drawing anxiety, inappropriate drawings (moderation of images is not solved; for a family product use rule-based hiding by host veto rather than ML). Large effort for the web bundle. Mitigation: ship after #1-#3 and #5, with a family-only rating.

### 4.5 #5 Hakawati (pitch it)

*Why.* Cheapest to build and a strong fit for a culture that loves storytelling and banter (a hakawati is the traditional coffee-house storyteller). Patently Stupid is called out by [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/every-jackbox-games-party-packs-best-ranked/) as a standout of PP5, where everyone gets a turn to shine; Talking Points was weaker, which tells us the improv has to be *scaffolded*, not blank-page.
*Core loop.* Each player gets two cards (an object and a customer or situation, e.g. "an umbrella" and "a bride in July"), 20 s to prepare, 30 s to **pitch aloud**; the room scores by tapping 1-5 stars (or a funniest vote). Final: pitch the mash-up of the two lowest-scoring cards. The TV shows cards, timer and a big applause meter.
*The laugh moment.* The forced mash-up in the final, and the TV "sold!" stamp with confetti.
*Difference.* All our other games run on text or taps; this is speech-first. Unlike Mamnou3! no one guesses and nobody is on a team.
*Platform fit.* Phases are cards, timers, stars: close to Most Likely's engine. Can reuse Most Likely's vote phase and content adapter.
*Content.* 100 object cards plus 60 situation cards per language, with Family and Spicy packs. Cards are short nouns, so translation is feasible, but jokes should be native.
*Risks.* Shy players (offer "skip for 0" and pair-pitch mode); audio-only content cannot be moderated, which is a feature for families; dependence on a lively host. Rank last because the laughs depend on the group more than on the game.

## 5. Comparison and sequencing

| | Zajal Battle | Mamnou3! | Qal el Jamaa | Rasmet-ha | Hakawati |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Typing | short | none | none | none | none |
| Draw on phone | no | no | no | **yes** | no |
| Works at 3 players | yes | needs 4+ (teams) | needs 5+ for stats | yes | yes |
| Works at 12 | yes | yes (3 per team) | yes | yes (long) | yes (long) |
| Text-heavy | low | low | low | none | none |
| Cultural risk | dialects, humour | low | stats accuracy | low | low |
| Replayability | very high | high | medium (content-bound) | high | medium |
| Build effort | M | M | M | L | S |

**Suggested order.** Build Hakawati first only if speed matters (S); otherwise ship Zajal Battle (the proven winner), then Mamnou3!, then Qal el Jamaa, then Rasmet-ha. Content writing, not code, is the bottleneck: each game needs native prompts in four languages, so reuse the Most Likely content pipeline and the Family/Spicy gate.

## 7. Originality checklist

- No Jackbox names (Quiplash, Fibbage, Drawful, Tee K.O., Blather 'Round, Guesspionage, Patently Stupid and so on are theirs); our names are Arabic words and phrases.
- No Jackbox prompts, host lines or art; prompts are written natively and host lines are in our own character voice.
- Mechanics (duel vote, Taboo-style description, crowd-percentage guessing, draw-and-pick, pitch-and-rate) are genre conventions; the study's legal section 6 explains why that is acceptable. Get a native review of every Arabic prompt before launch.
